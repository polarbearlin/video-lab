SpongeBob sparks online war between Turks and Chinese on X – Chinese praise Greece
A meme war between Turkish and Chinese users on X, triggered on 19 October by a Patrick Star image beside the Turkish flag, drew in thousands of accounts as Chinese users voiced support for Greece.
Photo: newsfire.gr
Eleni Papadaki-Van Der Merwe
Staff Writer
OCTOBER 22, 2026 AT 8:31 PM
Updated: Oct 23, 2026 12:12 PM
The clash began on 19 October when Turkish user ArdaaZz, who had around 85 followers at the time, posted a meme depicting a cat wearing a straw hat and thin moustache. The bold post immediately provoked a reaction from Chinese user LeLe (@LeLeluax), who uploaded an image of Patrick Star parachuting next to the Turkish flag. The image ironically implied that the national symbol of the crescent and star originated from the cartoon character, garnering over 9 million views and triggering mass Turkish responses.

The Great Wall and the “history lesson”
Turkish users counterattacked with historical references, arguing that the Great Wall of China was built out of fear of their ancient warriors against the nomads of the Steppe. The Chinese side responded by reminding them of the geographic distance between modern Turkey and the Wall, bringing to the forefront historical evidence about the defensive structures of Chinese dynasties against the nomadic Xiongnu.
Thousands of accounts caught in the dispute
The discussion quickly escalated beyond the bounds of humour. Thousands of accounts began exchanging insults about the economy and IMDb ratings. Users created fantasy alliance maps, presenting countries as opposing military blocs. Japanese, Taiwanese, and Indian accounts entered the fray, adding new dimensions to the online war.
The deconstruction of the Turkish national narrative
Chinese users decided to directly challenge the genetic and historical continuity of modern Turks with the ancient nomads of the Steppe. The dominant Chinese argument was clear: modern inhabitants of Turkey are essentially native Anatolians, Turkified Byzantine Greeks and Caucasians who were linguistically Islamised. They made it clear that the ancient Turks of the Steppe are one thing and modern Turkey is another.
Characteristic was the post by account @Adolf_KitlerXP:
“As a Chinese, I absolutely support Greece. Why? Because the Chinese hate invaders and colonizers. Turkey is a country founded by invaders. Many modern ‘Turks’ are Turkified Greeks who deny their true heritage”
.
The unexpected Sino-Greek internet alliance
The Chinese internet was flooded with pro-Greece posts. Several Chinese users recalled the critical Greek diplomatic assistance for the passage of the aircraft carrier Varyag through the Bosporus Straits.
“The three ancient civilizations – China, Greece and Iran – unite to intimidate Turkey”
, wrote account @Lostlanding, referring to a real geopolitical thriller of 2001.
The real historical backdrop: the Varyag in 2001
During that period, Turkey held the unfinished, 300-metre-long aircraft carrier Varyag trapped. Ankara blocked the passage of the adrift vessel through the Bosporus for a full 16 months, citing navigation dangers and fears of collision with Constantinople. To finally give the green light on 1 November, it demanded harsh diplomatic guarantees, enormous financial compensation, and Beijing’s explicit commitment to send two million tourists annually.
As soon as the colossus exited into the Aegean, the development turned into a maritime tragedy. The towed hull was struck by a severe 10 Beaufort storm. The towing cables broke. The vast ship began to drift uncontrolled near Skyros, on a dangerous course towards the coasts of Euboea. During the desperate attempt to reconnect the ropes amid the storm, one sailor fell into the sea and was killed. Rescue crews managed to regain control only on 6 November. The salvage of the ship allowed it to continue its long journey to Asia, where it was fully reconstructed and commissioned as the Liaoning, the first aircraft carrier in the history of the Chinese navy.
